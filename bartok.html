Bartók's Info

Overview
  • Refill his water bowl
  • Make sure his food dispenser has a blue light
  • In the evenings, give him Zyrtec with peanut butter
  • Check that nothing is obstructing the doggie door
  • Glance at the yard / around the first floor for any safety issues
Water

Bartók's water bowl is the red bowl to the left of the automatic food dispenser in the white cabinet in the left rear corner of the house.

You can either carry it to the sink to refill it or use a container (like the large green jug) to bring water to it.

If it's half full or more, you can just top it off.

If it's close to empty and you bring it to the sink, toss out the last drops and give it a rinse before refilling.

Food

Bartók's automatic food dispenser is in the white cabinet in the left rear corner of the house.

The light should be blue, which indicates it has food inside and is not blocked from dispensing food.

If the light is red and there is kibble in the tray, please verbally encourage Bartók to eat his food. It drops in two stages, so the red light in this case probably means the second drop was blocked by him not eating the first.

If the light is red and there is no kibble in the tray, the machine is (nearly) out of food. Please refill it from the bag of food in the plastic wheeled container to the right of the white cabinet. There is a little knob atop the machine to allow you to unlatch and relatch the lid.

Medicine

Every evening, Bartók should get one Zyrtec pill with some peanut butter.

If we forgot to leave out a spoon for this, they are in the drawer of the left of the sink.

Bartók is very good about sitting for his pill and will politely lick up all the peanut butter and the pill. Watch carefully so you'll notice if the pill falls on the floor. You may need to rotate the spoon to an angle where his tongue can more easily scoop up the pill.

Front Door

I'll message you the code for the door.

To unlock, press Yale, the code, and then Yale again.

To lock when leaving, press Yale. Listen for the mechanism and then test to confirm you can't open the door.

Walkies!

Before you do anything else, grab a roll of poop bags!

Bartók's harness and leash should be hanging just inside the door on the wall to the left when you walk in.

I put 2 roll of bags in the bright green collapsible water bowl on the same hooks as the leash and harness inside the front door.

The harness is light blue and the leash is dark blue and has a "I'm friendly" wrapper on it.

He should sit to get his harness put over his head, but he might be too excited given that he'll be alone most of the weekend. Also, even if he does sit, he doesn't really like having the thing go over his head despite how happy he is to be leashed up and taken out. He doesn't resist per se, but he might turn his head away unexpectedly.

The harness should already have the leash attached, which helps in figuring out how the harness goes on. The leash is attached to a part that acts like a handle above/between his shoulders. If you hold the harness up by that handle, it's fairly(?) obvious which part goes over his head.

The part with the buckle goes around his chest, behind his front legs. If he managed to sit for the over-the-head part, he'll likely stand and wiggle for the clipping in under his chest, which honestly actually makes it easier.

By the way, Bartók's collar stays on 24/7 -- well at least whenever he has doggie door access or is out of the house.

He would enjoy walking just about anywhere for any amount of time (I mean maybe not like a 5-hour hike...) but around the block, just up the street, whatever.

Most dog walking etiquette these days involves avoiding other dogs. Bartók is not as intense as Kenji is about alerting to other dogs and desperately wanting to meet them, but he will want to go towards them.

Between some leash tugs and a firm voice, Bartók can be redirected away from the other dog(s). If that doesn't do it, squat down and pet his neck, slowly, and use a calm, reassuring voice. I say things like "I know, baby. I'm sorry we can't meet the other doggies. It's gonna be okay. We need to go the other way now."

Depending on the route and time of day, you may not encounter any other dogs!

If Bartók does poop on the walk, you can put the used/full bag in Cooper's trash can in the alley on the side of this house.

He will definitely pee on A LOT of stuff.

If he's pulling a bit to start that will mellow out the longer you're walking. He's much better than he used to be about responding to tugs on the leash. Fully stopping and waiting until he slows down, backs up, and/or sits is a great option.
